Handheld Pixelsorter, called Harpy, is a device for making glitch art. Its whole purpose is pixel sorting, a glitch technique that reorders the pixels of a photograph by brightness into smeared, streaked bands, turning an ordinary image into structured noise and deliberate visual corruption in the tradition of glitch photography and databending. You point it at a subject, capture, and sort the image on the spot into fragmented, corrupted, glitched artwork.

It is a self-contained handheld gadget you hold in one hand, built on a Raspberry Pi 5 with a small vertical touchscreen and a camera, booting straight into its own sorting interface with no laptop, app or desktop, and running on battery for full portability. Physically it is a chunky off-white 3D printed plastic enclosure with visible printed layer lines and the word HARPY embossed on the case. The workflow is immediate: photograph a subject, choose a sorting direction, horizontal, vertical or diagonal, drag a threshold slider to control where the pixels break into streaks and segments, optionally tint by hue, crop, and export to USB, then feed the result back in for deeper, layered glitch fragmentation. The software is written in Rust with the egui framework, cross compiled for the Pi, and is open source under an MIT license.
